Owning Land

A classic principle of real estate is that buildings depreciate in value while the land appreciates in value. To build real long-term equity, owning the land is key. This is why townhomes and condos, which share land ownership and have high monthly fees, rarely build wealth as effectively as a single-family home on a dedicated lot.

HOAs

When comparing a single-family home to a townhome or condo, Homeowner Association (HOA) fees are often overlooked. In reality, they are a significant drain on your monthly budget and long-term borrowing power.

Reduced Borrowing Power

Banks approve loans based on your total monthly payment. A typical $195/month HOA fee eats into your budget, reducing your mortgage borrowing power by roughly $25,000 to $30,000. You pay the same monthly amount, but own less actual property.

Zero Equity Return

Every dollar spent on HOA fees is gone forever. Over 5 years, a $195/month fee adds up to $11,700 in pure expense. That is money that could have been going directly toward paying down our mortgage and building real home equity.

No Control Over Costs

HOA boards manage community funds but often lack the incentive to minimize costs. Homeowners frequently face rising fees due to inefficient vendor contracts, management overhead, or neighborhood projects you might not agree with.

Unpredictable Fee Hikes

While a fixed-rate mortgage keeps your principal and interest payments predictable, HOA fees can rise at any time. Boards can increase monthly dues or issue sudden, expensive "special assessments" to cover underfunded community repairs.

By choosing a single-family home with no HOA, we keep full control over our expenses and make sure our hard-earned money builds our own future.

The Time-in-Market Principle (Compounding Velocity)

In real estate, the most powerful driver of wealth is time. The greatest risk is not co-ownership; it is **waiting on the sidelines**. By pooling our resources, we start building equity immediately rather than letting rising prices outpace our savings.

The Cost of Waiting

If home values appreciate at a modest 4% annually, a $375,000 starter home gains $15,000 in value in just one year. Waiting 5 years to purchase alone means you may pay $75,000+ more for the exact same home, while having paid zero dollars toward your own mortgage principal.

The Compounding Advantage

By starting today with a combined down payment, we instantly activate the compounding wheel. Every monthly payment acts as a forced savings plan, building real wealth from day one that we can leverage for our future homes.

Asset Class Arbitrage: Premium vs. Starter Homes

In Utah's housing market, entry-level properties are in the highest demand. This creates a market imbalance where starter homes are overpriced relative to their actual quality. By teaming up, we step into a higher price tier where competition is lower and quality is exponentially higher.

Outsized Quality Premium

Premium homes (built post-2020) feature modern construction, energy-efficient HVAC systems, newer roofs, and turnkey finishes. We bypass the immediate repair backlogs and hidden renovation costs typical of starter homes.

Appreciation Stability

Premium single-family homes in quiet, spacious subdivisions hold their value better during market shifts and enjoy stronger, more resilient historical appreciation compared to denser developments.

The ADU Resiliency Buffer (Safety & Cash Flow)

Our target property is a single-family home with a fully independent daylight basement apartment (ADU). This structural design provides complete privacy today, and serves as an incredible financial safety net for the future.

Built-in Cash Flow Cushion

If a life event requires a family to relocate early, the vacant suite can be rented out on the open market. The rental income acts as a powerful buffer that easily covers that suite's portion of the mortgage, keeping the partnership safe and stable.

Refinancing Power

Banks value properties with documented rental income histories. The ADU's lease history provides robust supporting income, making the 5-year refinance and buyout process smoother and easier to qualify for.

Co-Ownership Financial Leverage

Combining our resources maximizes our financial efficiency. By cooperating, we qualify for terms and savings that are simply out of reach for a single household buying alone.

Eliminating Costly PMI

A 20% down payment on a premium home requires significant cash. By pooling our capital, we easily reach the 20% threshold, completely eliminating Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI)—saving hundreds of dollars each month from being lost to bank fees.

Optimized Interest Rates

A stronger combined credit profile, larger down payment, and lower Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratio allow us to secure the most competitive mortgage rates, saving tens of thousands of dollars in interest over the life of the loan.

Shared Transaction Costs

Closing fees, inspections, appraisal costs, and title fees can add up to thousands of dollars. We split these transaction expenses 50/50, keeping more cash in our reserves for furnishing and move-in costs.

Resilient Borrowing Power

Teaming up provides a buffer. The combined income and shared mortgage liability mean both families have a stronger foundation, satisfying strict underwriting criteria easily.
