Setup

The living arrangements, organizational roles, and legal protections that govern our co-ownership.

Dual Living

This is a structured property co-ownership for two families to build equity while living independently, not as roommates. Each family gets their own private entrance, full kitchen, laundry, and utility controls.

To keep operations smooth, we divide logistics into two clear roles (which can rotate or delegate chores 50/50):

  • Financial Administrator: Handles joint accounts, bills, mortgage payments, and utility tracking with digital receipts.
  • Property Manager: Coordinates seasonal maintenance, lawn/snow upkeep, and repair quotes.

This isn't about living together as roommates. It is a structured property co-ownership designed to help both households build equity while enjoying complete space and privacy.

We will focus on homes that naturally offer two separate, fully private living areas—typically a main level home (Upstairs) and a fully finished daylight basement (Downstairs). Each household will have:

  • Private Entrances: Entirely separate doorways, ensuring complete privacy.
  • Complete Amenities: Each home must have its own full kitchen, laundry room, bedrooms, bathrooms, and living areas.

To keep things running smoothly and fairly, we will divide the property duties into two clear roles. The person filling the role is in charge of organizing things and reporting progress while the other Co-Owner is in charge of auditing them (to double check that it gets done). Just because a role is in charge of organizing doesn't mean that they cannot delegate routine chores 50/50. After a perfect split (normally a rotation), the chores can be traded based on preference.

  • The Financial Administrator handles joint billing, mortgage payments, taxes, and utility tracking, providing clear digital receipts.
  • The Property Manager oversees seasonal maintenance, landscaping, HVAC checks, and coordinates any necessary repair quotes.
Ideal basement apartments have independent private entrances, daylight window layouts, and completely separate living zones.

Trust & Safeguards

Buying a home together is a big step. In any co-ownership, the greatest risks are unstated expectations. We want to align on all the details on paper first, ensuring complete peace of mind before we begin.

The Good Neighbor Agreement

Before we move in, we'll agree on and write down daily household details together to keep our neighborhood relationship positive and clear:

  • Driveway Parking: Assigned parking spaces and visitor guidelines.
  • Yard & Upkeep: Clear division of seasonal chores like mowing, weeding, and snow removal.
  • Pet Guidelines: Respectful boundaries for pet play areas. The Carters aren't planning on owning pets.
  • Quiet Hours: Agreed quiet times during weekdays and weekends.

Clear Legal Operating Agreement

A formal contract to clarify expectations, protect everyone's investment, and outline exit options:

  • Financial Transparency: Mutual credit and income validation to get the best financing.
  • Payment Safety Net (The Dynamic Ledger): If a family faces a temporary hardship, the other can step in to cover the mortgage. Instead of paying costly fees to constantly update the property deed, our Operating Agreement acts as a legally binding internal ledger of the new equity percentage which affects the final exit amounts (but not the monthly payment amounts).
  • Default Protections: To prevent foreclosure, if a family defaults repeatedly, a formal buyout process is triggered.
  • Buyout Protocol: Legally defined appraisal procedures, ownership percentages, and timeline triggers.
