Space & Privacy

Absolutely not. We are targeting a premium single-family home featuring a completely independent daylight basement apartment (ADU). Each family gets their own private exterior entrance, full kitchen, laundry room, bedrooms, bathrooms, living room, and separate thermostats/HVAC. We are neighbors sharing a wall, not roommates sharing a kitchen counter.

Neither family acts as a landlord, and neither will have access to the other's home. While there will likely be an interior door connecting the upstairs to the downstairs basement stairwell, it will be deadbolted from both sides. This guarantees that neither household can enter the other's home, maintaining absolute privacy and security for both families.

Both families will get at least two designated parking spaces. These spaces are factored directly into our professional rent appraisal, making sure that whoever gets more desirable parking (like garage access) pays a fair premium for it through our Rent Equalization formula. In the end, our ownership percentages can determine unequal parking setups—such as how a 3-car garage is split—keeping everything mathematically fair and balanced.

We will work together to set and finalize quiet hours together before signing our Operating Agreement. Our recommended arrangement is to observe quiet hours from 10:00 PM to 7:00 AM on weekdays, and from 11:00 PM to 8:00 AM on weekends.

Hosting friends and family is fully supported! Guests staying up to 14 consecutive days are completely welcome. To protect the privacy and comfort of the shared property, any guest staying longer than 14 consecutive days simply requires unanimous written consent from all co-owners.

Operations & Maintenance

To make sure chores are split fairly, we divide the work into two clear roles based on preference: a Financial Administrator (handles joint books, utilities, mortgage payments) and a Property Manager (oversees physical maintenance, landscaping, snow removal). While it's their responsibility to organize things, they can both delegate routine chores 50/50. After delegating 50/50, the chores can be bartered around to suit preferences (for example, if one family wants to mow in the summer and the other wants to shovel in the winter). If both families agree, any of the chores can be outsourced and split the cost (for example, hiring someone to mow the lawn).

Repairs to major shared structural systems are joint expenses. They are funded first by our joint Reserve Fund (which we maintain at a minimum of $10,000), and then split proportionally based on ownership percentages. This makes sure a broken water heater or roof leak never causes an unexpected financial emergency for either family.

Financial Safety Nets

Our agreement features a built-in Payment Safety Net (Dynamic Ledger). If a family experiences a temporary hardship, the other family can step in to cover the mortgage. Our legal Operating Agreement acts as an internal ledger, adjusting the final exit equity payout percentages down to the penny. This protects everyone's credit profile and makes sure everything is completely fair without incurring expensive legal fees to change the property deed.

If a single co-owner marries, the new spouse is formally integrated into the agreement within 90 days. They are added to the title and sign an addendum to the Operating Agreement, ensuring they carry the same rights, protections, and responsibilities as all other co-owners.

Life can throw curveballs. If a severe life event requires relocation or selling the home before the 5-year term ends, the exiting family provides 90 days' written notice. The remaining family has three main options. First, they have the first option to buy out their equity at full fair market value (established by a professional third-party appraisal). Or, they can take over all monthly expenses and buy out the other party within 6 months once an 80% LTV buyout is possible. Finally, if they decide not to buy out or take over monthly payments, the home will be listed 90 days after the notice and must be sold for any offers that match or exceed appraisal price. These three options allow flexibility for both families and a smooth, friendly transition.

We will hold title as Tenants in Common (TIC). TIC allows us to record unequal ownership shares (such as 60/40) on the deed to match our exact initial down payments. Each family retains complete control over their share, meaning it can be passed on to heirs in a will rather than automatically transferring to the other owner.

Holding title as a TIC is also very important for financing. It allows us to get standard home loans with great terms, whereas using an LLC forces the mortgage into commercial territory—meaning much higher interest rates, massive 20-25% down payments, and shorter terms. The TIC deed combined with our private Operating Agreement gives us the best of both worlds: residential interest rates and complete legal protection.

The 5-Year Exit Strategy

To solve this, we use the Valuation Buyout Protocol (sometimes called "buy-cut-choose"). One family suggests a single total valuation for the home. The other family then has the absolute right to either buy out the first family's share at that price, or sell their own share to them at that same price. Because the person setting the price doesn't know which side of the deal they'll end up on, they are naturally encouraged to name a perfectly fair, market-accurate number.

The staying family will buy out the departing family's equity share based on a fair market valuation (established by a professional third-party appraisal). The leaving family receives their full equity share (their down payment, plus their share of appreciation and mortgage principal paid off) in cash, giving them a clean, funded launch into their next home.

To make sure the departing family transitions smoothly, if they have maintained a perfect payment history over the 5 years, the staying family agrees to help them qualify for their new home purchase by serving as a co-signer if needed. The staying family leverages their own income along with the rental value of the newly vacant suite to help the departing family secure great financing for their next home.

If neither family wants to execute a buyout at Year 5, the families can mutually decide to continue status quo indefinitely. If either of the families want to list the home, it will be prepared for an open-market sale with a professional real estate broker. Any offer that meets or exceeds the appraised value will automatically be accepted unless both families reject the offer. Net proceeds from the sale are distributed by returning each family's Capital Account balance first (their initial down payment plus any value-add improvements), and then dividing the remaining profits strictly by ownership percentages.
