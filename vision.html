Vision

A shared strategy to pool resources, get more home per dollar, live privately, and build long-term wealth together.

Combining Power

By pooling our resources to buy a $750,000 home rather than two $375,000 starter homes, we bypass intense bidding wars and unlock far higher quality and value for every dollar spent:

  • Lower Competition: Entry-level starter homes are heavily crowded and overpriced; premium tiers face much lower buyer demand.
  • Outsized Returns: Pushing past the starter baseline gets us double the square footage, newer construction, and minimal immediate repairs.
  • Private Autonomy: A premium property accommodates a completely separate, daylight basement suite (ADU) so we live as neighbors, not roommates.

We don't have to wait decades or compromise on safety and comfort to own a home we love. By pooling our resources, we can step into a beautiful, move-in-ready home in a great neighborhood right now, skipping the competitive and overpriced starter-home market.

This works because of a simple market reality: home quality does not increase at the same rate as the price. In Utah's current market, entry-level single-family homes (around $375,000) are in the highest demand, meaning buyers pay a premium for properties that are often older, smaller, or need a lot of repairs.

If we combine our budgets to target a $750,000 home, the value we get is far more than double. We gain access to much larger layouts, higher-quality construction, and quieter neighborhoods. While a massive pool of buyers fights over the cheapest entry-level properties, larger homes with built-in private suites (Downstairs Apartments) remain much more reasonably priced. Together, we can buy a better home, on better terms, without the bidding wars.

A Tale of Two Homes

$375,000

Typical Budget Home

The high-demand starter tier where buyers face intense bidding wars for compromised properties.

  • Older Construction: Built 1940s–1980s; needs repairs or modern updates.
  • Limited Space: ~1,200–1,500 sq. ft. layouts with cramped bedrooms, low ceilings, and funny smells.
  • Poor Location: Less desirable neighborhoods, busier streets, aging infrastructure.
  • No ADU Potential: No extra space, lacks separate entrance, not enough parking.

$750,000 (Combined)

Premium Co-Owned Home

A luxury tier with far lower buyer competition and superior long-term appreciation.

  • Modern Quality: Move-in ready (built 2020s), premium finishes.
  • Expansive Layouts: ~3,000 – 3,500 sq. ft. with high ceilings and spacious lots.
  • Daylight Downstairs: Independent basement daylight apartment with private entrance, full kitchen, & laundry.
  • Quiet Subdivisions: Larger dedicated lot, quieter street, and better appreciation potential.

Co-Launch & Setup

Years 1–4

Enjoy & Build Equity

Year 5

We hire a professional appraiser to find the current market value. The staying family refinances to purchase the departing family's equity share in cash. The Co-Sign Guarantee helps the departing family qualify for their next home mortgage.

Social & Space Boundaries

  • Complete Privacy: Separate entrances, independent kitchens, and separate laundry rooms mean you are independent neighbors, not roommates.
  • Good Neighbor Rules: Assigned parking, pet guidelines, and agreed quiet hours keep daily life peaceful.
  • 48-Hour Openness Rule: Small annoyances are shared casually within 48 hours to prevent bottled-up feelings.

Staying Aligned & Comfortable

  • Conflict-Free Focus: We don't anticipate any conflicts—our shared values and strong friendship make us confident we'll align easily on daily life.
  • Casual Check-Ins: If a concern or question ever arises, we agree to sit down over dinner or coffee to chat openly in a relaxed, low-pressure environment.
  • Pre-Agreed Paths: If we ever need a structured way to resolve a question, our Operating Agreement outlines a simple, stress-free process so there is never any guesswork.

Financial Safety Nets

Life can bring unexpected changes. Our agreement is built to be highly protective, flexible, and supportive for both families so that no surprise ever threatens anyone's security or credit.

  • $10,000 Emergency Reserve: Covers shared structural repairs automatically so maintenance is never a source of stress between families.
  • Payment Safety Net (Dynamic Ledger): If one family faces a temporary hardship, the other can cover payments and track equity shifts internally, avoiding deed rewrite fees.
  • Early Hardship Exits: A pre-defined buyout process handles unexpected life changes (job relocation, illness, etc.) fairly.
  • Tenants in Common Ownership: Legally distinct ownership shares protect each family's capital and allow you to pass your share to heirs.

Emergency Reserve Fund

We maintain a joint reserve fund of at least $10,000 to cover major repairs. Emergency repairs are funded automatically, so unexpected maintenance never causes stress between families.

The Shifting Equity Ledger

If a family faces a temporary hardship, the other can step in to cover payments. The agreement acts as a legal internal ledger, adjusting exit equity percentages without deed rewrite costs.

Compassionate Hardship Exits

If a severe life event (job relocation, illness, divorce) requires an early exit, the contract provides a clear, pre-appraised path to sell the home and cash out equity safely.

Tenants in Common Ownership

We hold title as Tenants in Common. Each family's ownership share is distinct, fully protected, and can be passed on to heirs according to your wishes.

The Smart “Why”: Buy Land & Avoid HOAs

We choose this route because it is a smarter and stronger way to buy a home compared to buying a starter home or townhome alone. By focusing on a premium single-family home, we benefit from two basic principles of real estate:

  • The “Buy Dirt” Principle: We own the physical land under our home (which appreciates), unlike townhomes or condos that share land and grow equity slower.
  • Bypassing HOA Fees: Eliminating Homeowner Associations redirects hundreds of dollars every month straight into paying down our mortgage principal.

The “Buy Dirt” Principle

Structures (appliances, roofs, finishes) depreciate over time, but the land underneath appreciates. Townhomes and condos share land ownership and appreciate slower. By pooling budgets, we buy actual land in premium subdivisions, accelerating our long-term wealth.

Avoiding HOA Fees

HOA fees are non-equity costs that reduce your borrowing power by $25,000–$30,000. By choosing a single-family home with no HOA, we redirect hundreds of dollars monthly directly into paying off our mortgage, building our own future.
